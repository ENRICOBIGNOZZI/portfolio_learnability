THREE EMPIRICAL EXPERIMENTS — SECTION VI ONLY
Reference manuscript: Portfolio_Paper (27).pdf. No other section changed.
Real JKP stock-level managed payoffs; no simulated empirical observations.

1. HOW MUCH COMPLEXITY CAN BE USED PROFITABLY?
The Gaussian representation, 10,000 fixed features, initial bandwidth and
120-candidate penalty grid are unchanged. Forty-seven annual expanding refits
produce five non-overlapping OOS paths for every fixed penalty. The selected
policy uses prior five-year validation and is reported separately.

Decision block       Selected SR    Mean selected C    Descriptive peak C
1978–1989                 5.498               55.6                 154.1
1990–1999                 5.813              156.2                 145.1
2000–2009                 2.812              152.1                 130.6
2010–2019                 2.978              187.3                 288.4
2020–2024                 0.759               45.9                  16.1

Decision years correspond to February through the following January payoffs.
The peak is chosen retrospectively and cannot be used as an investment rule.
Training Sharpe rises sharply near interpolation, without matching OOS gains.
The last period favors much less complexity, but has only 60 OOS months and
wide uncertainty. A unique, stable optimal active complexity is not established.

2. DOES MORE HISTORY ACTIVATE MORE USEFUL DIRECTIONS?
The comparison uses exactly the same 384 OOS months (February 1993–January
2025) for T=60,120,240,360. Each history has a common 20-month validation
holdout, then refits on all T months. Fixing this holdout prevents validation
length from being an additional difference across T. It differs explicitly
from the five-year holdout in experiments 1 and 3.

T                    60         120         240         360
Mean C            34.36       59.94      125.79      170.07
OOS SR             2.367       2.488       2.750       2.629
Response-one L     0.7780      0.7573      0.6960      0.7270

The C360-C60 difference is positive in 28 of 32 years; its average is 135.7
with a paired three-year block interval [94.2,177.0]. More history generally
activates more directions. Performance is not monotone. The full-period
T360-T240 Sharpe difference is -0.121 with interval [-0.443,0.194].

The 1993–1999 block shows a loss increase when expanding 240 to 360 months;
2010–2019 shows substantial gains from expanding 60 to 240 months. In the
2020–2024 block every history has response-one loss above one, worse than a
zero-payoff benchmark. Local top-ten concentration changes but explains little
of the variation in the complexity gain (descriptive correlation -0.049).
The experiment cannot separately identify an observation-count benefit and
structural staleness. No global spectral exponent or Gaussian polynomial rate
is fitted. Grid boundaries bind in 28%,19%,16%,16% of annual fits across T,
so these are results of the frozen candidate procedure, not global optima.

3. WHAT LIES BEHIND THE ECONOMIC SPECTRUM?
Eigenvectors, signs, normalization and portfolio coefficients use training
data only. Disjoint groups cover ranks 0–10%,10–50%,50–90%,90–100% of positive
training eigenvalues. Near ties are not split. Diagnostic baskets have equal
training second moment; actual ridge policy contributions retain their fitted
magnitudes. These objects are kept separate throughout.

The leading group carries 99.921% of the training second-moment mass. The next
group carries only 0.068% but contributes 2.76 percentage points to the annual
policy mean. The last two groups' mean contributions are small and uncertain.
Nested policy Sharpe is 3.012,3.284,3.286,3.300; response-one loss is
0.5977,0.5617,0.5618,0.5587. The final group's small loss improvement includes
negative OOS covariance; treating the groups as independent would miss it.
The first intermediate group's loss improvement is not significant under the
paired pointwise interval; the final improvement is small and exploratory.

The six French factors explain only 2–8% of diagnostic OOS variance. The
leading basket has a positive profitability loading and the 50–90% group a
negative momentum loading, with HAC intervals excluding zero. Other loadings
are mostly uncertain. Full-sample regression residuals and intercepts are
distinguished from next-year residual payoffs using factor slopes fitted only
on past data. Low factor R-squared is not proof of a new risk premium.

Actual group policy coefficients have adjacent-refit cosines about 0.91–0.94:
the analysis does not establish pervasive unstable ridge weights. Equal-risk
diagnostic coefficient norms are much larger in the tail, indicating weak
direction amplification, not measured stock turnover. The weakest group has
an uncertain standalone mean and contributes limited diversification.

INFERENCE AND MATERIAL LIMITATIONS
All Sharpe ratios are annualized from monthly returns. Payoff loss is unscaled;
reported economic means/risks use the existing constant kappa=0.0374000.
There is no new leverage constraint or ex post volatility normalization.
Returns are before trading and borrowing costs.

Paired circular bootstrap: 5,000 draws, 12-month blocks; three-year blocks for
annual complexity. Sensitivity tables use 6/24 months and 2/5 years. These
conditional pointwise intervals do not re-estimate the entire learning pipeline
and are not simultaneous or adjusted for multiple testing. The final five-year
block and highly overlapping training histories require caution. The strongest
qualitative findings persist under the reported block-length checks.

The canonical pipeline excludes unresolved future returns before ranks and
the cross-sectional denominator are constructed. The prior audit identifies
7,489 affected stock-months. Characteristics and factor data are retrospective
snapshots, not certified historical vintages. Therefore the very high early
Sharpes and factor alphas are conditional backtest evidence, not a certified
implementable performance record. Missing payoffs have not been invented.

Computational scope: one frozen 10,000-feature Gaussian map; numeric positive
rank threshold 1e-12 of the leading eigenvalue; no new Matérn exercise, no
full model-refitting bootstrap, no trading-cost estimates for spectral baskets.
The existing managed cache avoids rebuilding multi-million-row stock panels.
Shared portfolio, chronology and provenance utilities are reused directly.
The full Gaussian policy reproduces the current repository's monthly payoffs
with maximum absolute discrepancy below 8.8e-11. Its Sharpe is 3.299903;
the reference manuscript's older 3.350 snapshot is not a calibration target.
Nineteen targeted numerical and chronology checks passed, including independent
direct ridge solves on actual early and late history-comparison windows.
