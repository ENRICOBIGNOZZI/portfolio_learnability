ECONOMIC INVESTMENT STRATEGIES IN THE MANAGED-PORTFOLIO SPECTRUM
Actual ridge-selected holdings; existing empirical experiment, 9 October 2026

WHAT IS BEING LEARNED?

The strongest directions implement a broad, relatively stable combination of
cash profitability, quality, momentum and value. The next 10–50% of spectral
ranks makes financing-related and conditional stock-weight adjustments that
partly offset this dominant portfolio. It adds expected payoff with substantial
covariance offsets. The deepest directions contribute little precisely measured
standalone mean return; some provide small diversification benefits. This is
learning refinements to a multivariate policy, not identifying each eigenvector
with a unique new anomaly.

Gross is each component's long-plus-short notional, not additive account

than selecting profitable characteristic buckets.

WHY THE 10–50% GROUP MATTERS

Its 0.0685% training second-moment mass is not its investment scale. Average long
and short notionals are 61.48% and 64.81% of NAV. Longs contribute 6.00 annual
percentage points and shorts -3.24, for 2.759 overall (pointwise 95% block
interval 1.504–4.066). Summing stock weights before taking absolute values shows
that it raises account gross from 226.68% to 233.28%, only 6.60 points: much of
its activity modifies the same stocks already held by the strong group.

Its own annual variance is 0.001547, while twice its covariance with the
preceding policy is -0.001269. Covariance offsets approximately 82% of own
variance; total variance nevertheless increases by 0.000278. Nested Sharpe
rises from 3.012 to 3.284. Response-one loss falls by 0.0360, but its paired
interval [-0.0941, 0.0294] includes zero. Positive component mean is better
established here than incremental portfolio-loss improvement.

The holdings favor increased financial liabilities (+16.15 input percentile
points long minus short) and lower changes in net financial assets (-17.43).
The debt-financing family tilt is +12.06 points, whereas profitability,
quality, momentum, and value tilts are -7.60, -8.55, -5.18, and -3.95 points.
Investment is +5.36 points. Equity issuance is negative: increased debt
financing and share issuance must not be conflated.

Characteristic terciles partition the actual positions, without new portfolios.
Low-profitability stocks contribute 2.04 annual percentage points, low-momentum
stocks 1.86, and recent losers 1.99. These sets overlap: DO NOT ADD THEIR PAYOFFS.
Low/middle/high financing terciles contribute -0.11/1.42/1.45 points. High
financing allocates -0.0518 to incremental loss and -0.000406 to annual
variance; low financing allocates +0.0361 and +0.000571, respectively.

Joint financing–profitability cells temper the additive interpretation.
High-financing/low-profitability and low-financing/high-profitability cells
have signed relative weight densities +0.251 and -0.502. Their annual payoffs
are +0.623 and -0.600 percentage points. Holding-weight nonadditivity is
described by formation-date cell projections, not return-model estimation;
these numbers are not evidence of a separately identified interaction premium.

ESTABLISHED FACTOR RISKS VERSUS INCREMENTAL VALUE

Regressions use the actual OOS policy contribution, not equal-risk diagnostic
eigenportfolios. The market/SMB/HML/RMW/CMA/MOM factors explain 17.90%, 7.14%,
1.83%, and 4.32% of return variance across the four groups. The strong group has
positive HML, RMW and MOM exposures. The second group's HML loading is -0.104
(12-lag HAC interval -0.194 to -0.013); other slope intervals include zero.

The second group's annualized descriptive intercept is 3.55% (HAC interval
1.94–5.16%), and factor mean contributions sum to approximately -0.79 points.
The existing training-fitted factor hedge leaves 3.60% annual mean payoff
(block interval 2.28–4.96%). Those are different estimates with different
timing; neither is evidence that omitted risks or implementation costs vanish.
The full period-by-period, factor-by-factor accounting is in the tables.

The 50–90% group contributes almost no marginal loss improvement. The final
group's mean is imprecise, but it lowers variance by about 0.000045 and loss
by 0.0031 (unadjusted pointwise interval -0.0071 to -0.0001). This is small
diversification value, not a well-established autonomous return strategy.

HISTORICAL STABILITY

First-to-last-period cosines of the 13 core family exposure profiles are
0.914, 0.700, -0.186, and 0.443. The leading group preserves all family signs.
Group 10–50% preserves positive investment and negative profitability/momentum
in all period averages, but its value tilt changes sign. Its financing tilt
is 25.0, 16.2, 2.3, 4.6, and 7.5 points across the five existing periods.
Component gross is 98.7%, 178.7%, 145.2%, 134.5%, and 33.4%. Composition and
intensity both change. The 50–90% group's economic content is especially
unstable; stable feature coefficients would not guarantee a stable economic
interpretation when the underlying distribution changes.

METHOD AND ACCOUNTING

All 2,198,755 OOS stock-months, 47 annual refits, and 564 monthly payoffs use
the unchanged representation, selected penalties, annual grouping, feature map,
stock denominator and common scale. The January decision uses payoffs already
known at that January close; monthly holdings use formation characteristics.
Subsequent returns never determine bins, characteristic signs, or stock lists.
Near-tied spectral boundaries follow the existing grouping. The four weight
columns reconstruct the original stock policy and group payoffs numerically.
Maximum absolute stock-weight error is 2.19e-13 and monthly group-payoff error
is 5.85e-13. The 17 relevant holdings and prior-experiment tests pass.

For characteristic x and group weights w, L=sum(max(w,0)), S=sum(max(-w,0)).
Long mean=sum(max(w,0)*x)/L; short mean=sum(max(-w,0)*x)/S. Their difference
is scale-free; actual signed exposure is L*long mean-S*short mean. All use
the existing [-0.5,0.5] input ranks, including neutral-zero missing inputs.
Family scores average signed ranks; positive means MORE of the attribute,
not presumed higher expected return. The family membership dictionary is
recorded in audit/economic_protocol.json and financing_followup_design.json.

Terciles use contemporaneous average-rank ties, separately each month. Every
cell is retained. Each family or pair is an ALTERNATIVE exhaustive view of the
same positions, never a mutually exclusive decomposition across families.
Financing views were motivated by dominant holdings exposures and are
exploratory, not preregistered. They do not change any fitted stock weight.

Relative cell weight density = (mean signed stock weight in the cell) /
(mean absolute stock weight in the group). A stock-count-weighted additive
row/column projection on the nine formation-date cell densities gives the
reported nonadditive residual. Empty cells have zero payoff but undefined
observed density; public density summaries omit those entries. This affects
120 group-cell-months in Size x Liquidity only; none of the displayed joint
partitions has empty cells. Immutable raw checkpoints retain zero accumulators
for empties, normalized explicitly by economic_postprocess.py before summaries.

For preceding raw payoff p, added raw group payoff q and cell payoff q_b,
loss allocation = mean[-2*(1-p)*q_b + q*q_b]. For scaled excess payoffs,
annual variance allocation = 12*[2*Cov(p,q_b)+Cov(q,q_b)]. These allocations
sum to group increments and retain cross-covariances. They are an accounting
convention, not the causal effect of independently removing a cell. Common-
sample factor coefficients also add exactly across cells; regression R-squared
does not. Mean and loss intervals use 5,000 paired circular 12-month blocks;
factor intervals use 12-lag HAC. All intervals are pointwise and conditional
on the fitted policy paths, with no multiple-testing or causal interpretation.

The retrospective JKP academic dictionary and the existing complete-payoff
universe are accepted inputs. No anomaly-discovery or publication-date
investigation, new portfolio learner, or feature-selection exercise is added.

FILES AND REPRODUCTION

publication/Section_VI_Economic_Investment_Strategies.pdf is the standalone
replacement Section VI. The prior A/B experiments are reused unchanged. It
preserves the reference-derived typography and manuscript notation; the rest
of the manuscript is never written. The reference binary was unavailable at
this build, so compatibility uses its retained extracted text and prior
Section VI template rather than a new binary comparison.

figures/ contains four vector PDFs and high-resolution PNGs:
  EC1: actual holdings, long-minus-short exposures, and leg payoffs
  EC2: economic content across the five historical periods
  EC3: joint characteristic content of group 10–50%
  EC4: mean, loss, and covariance-aware variance attribution
The same images are copied into IMMAGINI PER CHAT/outputs/
spectral_economic_content_20261009/ with a gallery index.

tables/dominant_long_short_exposures_named.csv reports named long/short
exposures for all four groups and six periods (full sample plus five blocks).
monthly_characteristics.parquet contains every monthly exposure for all 130
inputs; annual_characteristics.csv provides annual means. Family, cell,
factor, joint-weight, stock-netting and stability tables preserve both monthly
inputs and period summaries. Financing follow-up tables carry that prefix.
factor_mean_accounting.csv separates each factor's contribution to mean return
from the descriptive intercept. audit/ records input, code and output hashes.

Every actual stock position is in local results/spectral_economic_content_20261009/
year_YYYY/holdings.parquet, with formation_date, return_date, verified CRSP
PERMNO, four signed group weights, and total_weight. holdings_index.html opens
latest-month long and short lists. latest_all_stock_holdings.csv contains all
latest-month stocks; top_positions_all_months.csv contains the top 20 on each
side for all months, selected only by absolute position magnitude. Company
names/tickers are unavailable in the retained extract. Licensed stock-level
files remain outside Git; the public outputs are group-level aggregates.
